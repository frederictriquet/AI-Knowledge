# Reports over RAG (RAG as a feature, not a benefit)

**In one sentence** — Liu predicts a shift from "question-answer" RAG toward **report generation**, because the value of a report (decision support) far exceeds the time saved finding an answer.

## What the source says
"RAG is the feature, not the benefit." According to Liu, Q&A RAG systems deliver **one-dimensional** value: the time saved finding an answer, hard to sell beyond that. A **report**, by contrast, is a decision tool: it is valued as a fraction of a high-leverage stake (allocating a budget, hiring), not as a fraction of an hourly wage. His example: the same "looping" RAG pipeline that produces an answer is worth a few hours of salary, but the **report** that results from it can be billed at $20,000 if it informs the allocation of a $5M budget. He stresses the role of **SOPs** (standard operating procedures): the *form* of the report — structured template, clear deliverables, objective, decision criteria, follow-ups — is what creates value, more than a raw transcript. He anticipates the emergence of a **market for report templates** and the fact that knowing how to define the right template will become a skill in itself.

## Example
Liu's hiring case: six interview rounds. The "question-answer" RAG version — you query the notes, it works, but the value stops there. The report version — a service ingests the notes from the six interviews and pours them into an established hiring template: "Has worked in a team before?", "Is he autonomous?", "Does he reflect our values?", generating a *hiring overview* usable by the committee. The value is no longer the time saved but the decision: a recruiter bills $40,000 on a $250,000 candidate. Same pipeline, a radically more monetizable deliverable.

## Why it matters
Liu shifts the question toward **product value**: what is the agent's output for? His thesis — design the agent to produce a structured, decision-oriented deliverable (report/SOP) rather than a one-off answer — is a strategic perspective complementary to approaches centered on the retrieval-reasoning mechanism.

## Key points
- "RAG is the feature, not the benefit": Q&A is one-dimensional (time saved).
- A report is valued as a % of a high-leverage outcome, not as a % of salary.
- Value comes from the *form* (SOP/structured template), not the raw transcript.
- Prediction: marketplaces of templates; defining the right template = key skill.
